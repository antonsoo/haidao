01 / Make a record

Observe first. Calculate second.

Model the source exercise with equal vertical poles on one level baseline and each eye on the ground. No angle is given or measured. Every length below is in bu.

Here 1 bu = 6 chi (尺), 1 zhang (丈) = 10 chi, and 1 li (里) = 300 bu. Thus the source's three-zhang poles are five bu high. These are units in the exercise; no conversion to metres is assumed. The following pine problem converts fifty bu to three hundred chi across openings 12–13.

02 / Operate the recipe

Why does checking the second sighting matter?

Put the front pole at x = 0, rear pole at x = s, and the island foot at x = −D. Each pole has height h. The ground eyes are at x = a and x = s + b.

Front line: y = h(a − x) / a

Rear line: y = h(s + b − x) / b

At the common peak: H = h(a + D) / a = h(s + b + D) / b

So D(b − a) = as; H = h + hs/(b − a).

For any nonzero divisor f, the recipe's candidate D = as/f and H = h + hs/f fits the front line exactly. The second line rejects it unless f = b − a. When b = a, the lines are parallel; when b < a, their intersection is behind the front pole and below pole height, outside the forward-island setup.

This coordinate derivation is modern. It explains the model here; it is not presented as Liu Hui's own proof.

Which witness is this?

Haidao suanjing, Waseda University Library ni02 00649, catalogued as 1777. The bibliographic preface is dated the fourth month of Qianlong 40 (1775). Its header attributes the work to Liu Hui of Jin and the commentary to Li Chunfeng of Tang. The work is associated with Liu Hui's third-century mathematics; this photographed Qing book is a much later printed witness.

The Qing preface says the material was compiled from the Yongle Encyclopedia. The contradictory wording in the commentary is what this witness transmits; we do not infer where that reading originated. The ten excerpts here are selected clauses, not a complete critical edition or a reconstruction of ancient pronunciation.
